[AM] Teach Us to Count Our Days

Good morning. Today is Saturday, November 7th. This is Daily Devotion. On a Saturday morning, the calendar is already packed full: places to go, people to meet, things to hand in, one square after another. What we fear most is not being busy; it is getting through a busy day, looking back, and not knowing what the day was actually for. First, hear a prayer of Moses — he did not ask for more days; he asked to count his days right.

Today's verse is Psalm 90, verse 12. Moses prays: So teach us to count our days, that we may gain a heart of wisdom.

Notice: what Moses asked for was not longer days, but to count our days. Once the days are counted, they carry weight: knowing they are limited, we do not spend our best strength on the least important things; knowing they pass by one after another, we first ask a question: is this day worth living this way? A heart of wisdom is not a sharp abacus; it is putting first things first — draw near to God first, be with family, keep what we promised others, and let the rest wait in line. Psalm 90 was written by Moses in the wilderness. He had seen too many days wasted away, so the wisdom he asked for begins with treasuring each day. Before you go out today, run this day through your heart once: what must be done, and what can be set down. The one who counts his days walks with steadier steps.

Let's pray together. Heavenly Father, our days are in Your hands. Teach us to count our days — not to waste the time You have given, nor to be dragged along by busyness — and give us a heart of wisdom, to do first what ought to be done first. In Jesus' name, Amen.

May the Lord teach you to count your days: busy, but not in disorder. See you tomorrow.
