[AM] John 4:23-24: Worship the Father in Spirit and Truth

Good morning. Today is Thursday, November 19th. This is Daily Devotion. Many people treat worship as the singing part of a service: the song ends, the gathering ends, and the feet are already heading out before the heart has come before God. Today Jesus' words remind us: the Father is not looking for a worship service; He is looking for people who worship Him.

Today's verse is John chapter 4, verses 23 to 24. The Scripture says: But the hour comes, and now is, when the true worshipers will worship the Father in spirit and truth, for the Father seeks such to be his worshipers. God is spirit, and those who worship him must worship in spirit and truth.

Bill Johnson, speaking of worship, said that God is not looking for the act of worship, but for the worshiper, because a person becomes like the one he worships. Jesus did not give this deepest revelation about worship to the religious leaders; He gave it to a Samaritan woman. Her life was in a mess, yet Jesus told her with His own mouth that the Father seeks such to worship Him. No one is disqualified from worship. And worship is not only in the gathering: a word of praise in your heart on the way to work, a quiet "I love You" while waiting at a red light, a word of thanks while cooking — all of this is worshiping Him in spirit and truth. What a person keeps looking at, the heart comes to resemble. Stare at difficulties all day, and the heart grows into the shape of the difficulties; turn the heart to the Father first in the morning, and today you will see people and things with different eyes. Worship first, then step out: when the heart is set right first, the steps will not be in disorder.

Let's pray together. Father, thank You for seeking us and not turning away from us. Make us worship You today in spirit and truth; in our work and in how we treat people, let us put our hearts before You first, and live out Your likeness. In Jesus' name, Amen.

May the Lord give you a heart of worship for the road today. See you tomorrow.
